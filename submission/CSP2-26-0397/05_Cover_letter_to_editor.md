Dear Editors,

Thank you for the opportunity to correct our submission. We resubmit manuscript CSP2-26-0397, "From Opinion Polls to Psychometrics: A Review and Practical Guide to Survey Design in Socio-ecological Research," with the literature-search methods now fully documented.

The manuscript was returned because it lacked details of the methods used to search the literature and a PRISMA flow diagram. We have addressed both. Following your guidance and the criteria in Pullin and Stewart (2006) and Page et al. (2021), the paper remains a **practice-focused review** — its contribution is the integration of dispersed methodological guidance into a single workflow, not the estimation of an effect — but the process by which the body of literature was assembled and curated is now explicit and reproducible.

Specifically:

1. **The Methods section has been substantially expanded** and retitled *Methods: literature search, screening, and curation*. It now states the review type and rationale, the databases and editions searched, the coverage dates and the date the search was executed, the language and document-type limits, the full search strategy, the eligibility criteria, the screening procedure and inter-screener agreement, and the explicit criteria used to curate among eligible sources.

2. **A new Figure 2 presents a PRISMA-2020-style flow diagram** documenting the flow of records from identification through screening, full-text assessment, curation, and inclusion, with exclusion counts and reasons at each stage. Because this is a practice-focused review, the diagram includes a dedicated curation stage showing how many eligible records were retained against prespecified criteria and how many were set aside — so that the curation is auditable rather than asserted. The former Figure 2 is now Figure 3.

3. **The Supplementary Material has been expanded** from a repository link to include Table S1 (all search strings verbatim, with limits and per-string retrieval counts), Table S2 (eligible records not carried forward, with reasons), Table S3 (included sources mapped to the twelve workflow steps), and Table S4 (a PRISMA 2020 item crosswalk indicating where each applicable item is addressed and which items do not apply to a narrative methodological synthesis).

4. **Search exports and the screening log are archived** in the accompanying open repository at `Relevant Literature/search_2026/`, and Pullin and Stewart (2006) and Page et al. (2021) are now cited in the Methods.

5. We have also expanded the limitations to name explicitly the biases our search introduces — restriction to [English]-language sources indexed in commercial databases, which under-represents practitioner knowledge and non-Anglophone scholarship. We consider this important to state plainly in a paper that advocates decolonial and community-engaged practice.

We hope the manuscript is now suitable for peer review, and we are glad to provide any further documentation the office requires.

Sincerely,

Alejandra Echeverri, on behalf of the authors
