## Methods: literature search, screening, and curation

Synthetic evidence indicates that socio-ecological research frequently under-utilizes psychometric and social-science theory. This is a practice-focused review. Our aim is not to estimate an effect or to characterise the prevalence of a practice, but to consolidate dispersed methodological guidance into a single workflow usable by ecologists designing socio-ecological surveys. 

Accordingly, our literature search followed two independent and complementary streams (Figure 2). **Stream A** used a top-down database search to identify systematically retrieved records. We searched Web of Science Core Collection (SCI-EXPANDED, SSCI, A&HCI) on July 30, 2026, with coverage from 1970 to 2026, limited to English-language Articles, Reviews, and Book Chapters. The core search (1,612 records) was supplemented by 11 topic-specific searches targeting individual workflow steps (15,761 records). After deduplication (n = 11,271) and screening for title/abstract availability (n = 11,237), records were screened using an automated keyword pre-screen (n = 8,276) and human-coder assessment. The human coder (A. Echeverri) retained 1,580 records for the working corpus, from which a random 25% subsample (n = 395) was drawn for full-text synthesis.

**Stream B** used a bottom-up snowball and expert-knowledge search to identify foundational texts, standard handbooks in survey methodology and conservation social science, and established reporting standards (n = 242). This stream captures knowledge accumulated across the author team's decades of designing, fielding, and publishing socio-ecological surveys, ensuring the inclusion of methodologically influential works (e.g., Stern, 2018; Dillman et al., 2014) and institutional documents (e.g., CARE, FAIR, PRISMA).

The two streams were merged by de-duplicating on DOI and title, identifying 32 overlapping records. This resulted in a final synthesis set of 605 sources included in the review. Because the object of synthesis is methodological guidance rather than comparable quantitative findings, we do not conduct meta-analysis, and we make no claim to exhaustive coverage; we instead aim for transparency and reproducibility in how the curated body of literature was assembled. Full search strings, export files, and the screening log are archived in the accompanying repository.

### Eligibility criteria

We included sources that provided methodological guidance, validated measurement instruments, or reporting standards relevant to designing, administering, analysing, or reporting surveys in socio-ecological, conservation, or environmental contexts. We excluded sources that were not methodologically informative for survey design; that offered no guidance transferable to ecological research contexts; that had been superseded by a more recent or more authoritative treatment of the same material; or whose full text was unavailable.

Because a practice-focused review must select among eligible sources rather than include all of them, we applied an explicit curation step to the pool of full-text-eligible records. We retained sources meeting at least one of three prespecified criteria: **methodological influence** — foundational or widely adopted treatments, including textbooks used in courses teaching social theory and survey design (e.g. Stern, 2018) and standards adopted by journals or professional bodies; **direct transferability** — guidance applicable without substantial modification to ecological and conservation research contexts; and **technique illustration** — provision of a concrete worked example of a specific method, scale, or analytic step. Eligible records not meeting any criterion were set aside `[n = XX]` and are listed in Supplementary Table S2, so that readers can see what was available but not carried forward.

Of 17,373 records identified in total from database searches (Stream A), 6,102 duplicates were removed, 11,237 were screened on title and abstract, and 1,580 were retained in the working corpus. Following the random 25% subsampling, 395 Stream A records were merged with 242 records from the bottom-up expert search (Stream B). After accounting for 32 overlapping records, 605 sources were included in the synthesis. Of these, a subset are cited in the main text and all are catalogued with their core constructs, strengths, and limitations in the living tables of the accompanying repository. Included sources were mapped to the twelve steps; per-step counts are given in Supplementary Table S3.

### Positionality and limitations of the curation

Our curation reflects the disciplinary training of the author team in conservation psychology and human dimensions of wildlife, and our search was restricted to [English]-language sources indexed in [two] commercial databases. Both choices bias the corpus toward Anglophone, Global North scholarship — a limitation that is especially consequential for a paper advocating decolonial and community-engaged practice, and one we cannot resolve within the present review. Practitioner knowledge held in unpublished reports, community protocols, and non-indexed regional journals is therefore under-represented relative to its methodological value. We treat the accompanying repository as a living document and invite contributions that redress these gaps; the tables of scales, theories, and reporting standards will be updated as the literature evolves.
